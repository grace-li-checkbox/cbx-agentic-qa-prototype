**Section 1 — Purpose and Scope**

This playbook establishes Verdantis Solutions Inc.'s standardized negotiation positions for non-disclosure agreements ("NDAs") executed in connection with potential mergers, acquisitions, divestitures, joint ventures, strategic investments, and other strategic transactions (each, a "Transaction"). It is designed to ensure consistency, efficiency, and appropriate risk management across all NDA negotiations in which Verdantis participates, whether as a potential acquiror, a potential target, or a co-participant in a strategic combination.

This playbook is applicable to all M&amp;A NDAs to which Verdantis Solutions Inc. (Nasdaq: VRDS) is or will be a party, regardless of the identity, size, or sophistication of the counterparty. The positions set forth herein apply with equal force whether Verdantis is disclosing or receiving confidential information (or both) and whether the Transaction involves a public or private counterparty, a domestic or cross-border opportunity, or an acquisition of equity interests, assets, or a business division.

This playbook supersedes all prior versions, including Version 4.1 (dated September 8, 2023) and all earlier iterations. It is effective as of March 15, 2024, and governs all NDA negotiations commenced on or after that date. For NDAs currently under negotiation as of the effective date, the deal team should consult the playbook custodian to determine whether transition to the Version 4.2 positions is appropriate.

All outside counsel engaged by Verdantis on M&amp;A NDA review — whether for a specific transaction or on a standing engagement — must receive a copy of the current playbook version prior to commencing any substantive NDA work on Verdantis's behalf. Outside counsel are expected to conform their advice, redlines, and negotiation strategy to the positions set forth in this playbook. Where a counterparty's draft deviates from the preferred or fallback positions described herein, outside counsel must identify and classify the deviation in accordance with the risk-tier framework set forth in Section 2 and seek appropriate client approval before agreeing to any non-preferred term.

Deviations from the playbook positions — including acceptance of a counterparty's non-conforming term or agreement to a position less favorable than the applicable fallback position — require prior written approval from the designated approval authority in accordance with the Approval Authority Matrix set forth in Section 2 below. No member of the Verdantis deal team or outside counsel is authorized to agree to a non-playbook position without obtaining such approval.

This document is protected by the attorney-client privilege and the work-product doctrine. Its distribution is limited to authorized Verdantis personnel and approved outside counsel who have executed appropriate confidentiality undertakings. This document shall not be shared with counterparties, counterparty counsel, financial advisors not engaged by Verdantis, or any other third party without the express prior written consent of the General Counsel.

**Section 1A — Who Reviews an NDA**

The business may not negotiate an NDA under this Playbook itself. Every NDA covered here protects information exchanged around a potential strategic transaction, where one loose word in a standstill or non-solicitation clause can cost more than the deal is worth. There is no self-serve band and no set of self-serve fallbacks.

There are two bands only. No review: Verdantis's standard-form mutual NDA, signed exactly as issued, for a preliminary discussion lasting no more than 90 days, at which no non-public financial information, customer lists, pricing or source code will be exchanged, and signed by a Vice President or above. Legal review: everything else, including any counterparty paper, any change to the standard form, any NDA that names a specific transaction or target, and any NDA requested by a banker or adviser on a bidder's behalf. Deviations rated High Risk under Section 2.1 also go to the General Counsel.

Contract value plays no part in this decision. An NDA carries no fee, so its price says nothing about its risk. If the requester cannot confirm what information will be exchanged, or for how long, the NDA goes to Legal review.

**Section 2 — Approval Authority Matrix**

All deviations from the Preferred Position stated in this playbook must be classified by risk tier and approved by the designated authority before Verdantis agrees to any non-preferred term. This framework is designed to ensure that the appropriate level of legal and business judgment is applied to each negotiation concession, commensurate with the magnitude of the risk involved, while also enabling the deal team to move efficiently on routine NDA negotiations without unnecessary escalation.

The risk-tier classification system, approval authorities, and procedural requirements set forth in this Section 2 apply to every provision of the NDA. Where a counterparty's draft contains multiple deviations, each deviation must be individually classified, but the overall approval authority required for execution of the NDA is governed by the highest applicable risk tier among all identified deviations.

**Section 2.1 — Risk-Tier Classification**

Each deviation from the Preferred Position set forth in this playbook shall be classified into one of the following three risk tiers:

**Low Risk.** Low Risk deviations are those that present minimal legal or commercial exposure and do not alter the fundamental risk allocation contemplated by the NDA. Low Risk deviations typically involve matters of form, convenience, or preference rather than substance. Examples of Low Risk deviations include, without limitation:

> • Governing-law or forum-selection variations among commercially reasonable alternatives (e.g., substitution of New York law for Delaware law, as described in Section 9.1);
>
> • Minor scope adjustments to warranty disclaimers that do not eliminate the fraud/intentional-misrepresentation savings clause;
>
> • Formatting or structural differences in the organization of the NDA that do not affect substantive rights or obligations;
>
> • Variations in notice provisions (e.g., alternative addresses, electronic notice mechanisms) that do not impair Verdantis's ability to receive timely notice of material events.

**Medium Risk.** Medium Risk deviations are those that materially expand Verdantis's obligations or restrict Verdantis's rights under the NDA but remain within the outer boundary of acceptable deal terms. Medium Risk deviations warrant careful legal review and business-judgment assessment by the General Counsel but do not, standing alone, create the level of exposure that would require involvement of the corporate development function or the Board of Directors. Examples of Medium Risk deviations include, without limitation:

> • Survival periods exceeding 2 years but not exceeding 3 years;
>
> • Return/destruction timelines that are compressed below the preferred 15-business-day period but are not fewer than 10 business days, or that omit one (but not both) of the standard retention carve-outs;
>
> • Omission of a residuals clause from the counterparty's draft;
>
> • Warranty disclaimers that purport to apply broadly to the NDA as a whole (rather than being limited to the warranty section) but do not purport to limit remedies for breach;
>
> • Requirements to provide destruction certification by a specific officer title (e.g., CEO or CFO) rather than any authorized officer.

**High Risk.** High Risk deviations are those that create significant legal exposure, restrict Verdantis's strategic flexibility, or implicate securities-law compliance obligations. High Risk deviations require the highest level of internal approval and, in certain cases, board-level notification before acceptance. Examples of High Risk deviations include, without limitation:

> • Acceptance of any standstill provision (regardless of duration or scope), which also triggers the board notification requirement described in Section 7.2;
>
> • Non-solicitation terms exceeding 12 months in duration or imposing liquidated-damages provisions for non-solicit breaches;
>
> • Stipulated-injunction provisions that waive the requirement to post a bond or to prove irreparable harm as a precondition to injunctive relief;
>
> • Omission of any of the five standard exclusions from the definition of Confidential Information, particularly the independent-development exclusion;
>
> • Absence of a securities-law carve-out in a non-disclosure-of-discussions clause, which creates a direct conflict between the NDA obligations and Verdantis's disclosure obligations as a Nasdaq-listed public company;
>
> • Definitions of "Representatives" or "Permitted Disclosees" that exclude external legal counsel, financial advisors, potential lenders, and potential co-investors;
>
> • Non-solicitation provisions with scope extending to any employee "contacted" during diligence (as opposed to those "introduced" in connection with the due diligence process).

**Section 2.3 — Approval Procedures**

The following procedures govern the documentation and processing of deviation approvals:

Outside counsel must flag each deviation from the playbook's Preferred Position in a clause-by-clause Issues Memorandum prepared in connection with each counterparty draft NDA. For each identified deviation, the Issues Memorandum must: (i) describe the deviation and identify the applicable playbook section; (ii) assign a risk tier in accordance with Section 2.1; (iii) assess the legal and commercial implications of the deviation; and (iv) recommend a negotiation approach (accept, counter with specific alternative language, or reject). The Issues Memorandum should be organized in descending order of risk tier, with High Risk deviations addressed first.

Approvals should be documented in writing and retained in the matter file maintained by the Verdantis legal department. Email approvals from the designated approval authority are acceptable and should include a reference to the specific deviation being approved and the risk tier classification. For High Risk deviations, the approval email or memorandum should also document the business rationale for acceptance and any mitigating terms negotiated in exchange for the concession.

If multiple deviations are present in a single counterparty draft, the highest applicable risk tier among all identified deviations governs the overall approval authority required for Verdantis to execute the NDA. However, each individual deviation must be separately classified, documented, and approved. The fact that a High Risk deviation exists in the draft does not relieve outside counsel of the obligation to classify and document Medium Risk and Low Risk deviations.

Outside counsel should not communicate any deviation approval to counterparty counsel, or agree to any non-preferred term in markup or negotiation, until the relevant internal approval has been obtained and confirmed in writing by the Verdantis legal team.

**Section 3 — Definition of Confidential Information**

The definition of "Confidential Information" is the foundation of any NDA and determines the scope of the obligations that flow from it. Verdantis's negotiation positions on the definition of Confidential Information are designed to ensure that the definition is appropriately broad to capture the range of information likely to be exchanged in an M&amp;A context, while also ensuring that the standard exclusions necessary to protect Verdantis's operational freedom and legal rights are preserved in every NDA.

**Section 3.1 — Standard Exclusions from Confidential Information**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA must contain all five of the following standard exclusions from the definition of "Confidential Information." These exclusions are fundamental to the proper functioning of the NDA and reflect well-established principles of confidentiality law. Verdantis will not execute an NDA that omits any of these exclusions without High Risk approval in accordance with Section 2.

The five required exclusions are as follows:

> 1. **Public Domain.** Information that is or becomes generally available to the public other than as a result of a breach of the NDA by the Receiving Party or its Representatives. This exclusion ensures that the Receiving Party is not burdened with confidentiality obligations with respect to information that is freely available to the public through no fault of the Receiving Party. The exclusion should be drafted to require that the information became publicly available through means other than a breach by the Receiving Party or any person to whom the Receiving Party disclosed the information.
>
> 2. **Prior Possession.** Information that was already in the Receiving Party's possession prior to disclosure by the Disclosing Party, as demonstrated by contemporaneous written records, and was not obtained directly or indirectly in violation of any confidentiality obligation owed to the Disclosing Party. This exclusion protects Verdantis from claims that information it independently possessed prior to the commencement of discussions constitutes the counterparty's Confidential Information. The requirement for contemporaneous written records provides an objective evidentiary standard and is market-standard.
>
> 3. **Independent Development.** Information independently developed by the Receiving Party or its Representatives without reference to or use of the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, as demonstrated by contemporaneous written records. This exclusion is critically important for a technology company like Verdantis, which maintains active research, development, and product-engineering programs. Without this exclusion, the mere fact that Verdantis's engineers develop a feature or capability that is similar to information disclosed by the counterparty could give rise to a claim of misappropriation, even if the development was entirely independent.
>
> 4. **Third-Party Lawful Receipt.** Information received by the Receiving Party from a third party that is not, to the Receiving Party's knowledge, subject to a confidentiality obligation to the Disclosing Party with respect to such information. This exclusion protects Verdantis when it receives information through lawful channels independent of the NDA relationship. The knowledge qualifier ("to the Receiving Party's knowledge") is important to prevent the exclusion from being rendered illusory by an argument that the third party was, unbeknownst to the Receiving Party, subject to a confidentiality obligation.
>
> 5. **Compelled Disclosure.** Information that is required to be disclosed by applicable law, regulation, legal process, or governmental order, subject to compliance with the notice and cooperation provisions set forth in Section 6 of this playbook. This exclusion ensures that neither party is placed in the position of having to choose between complying with its legal obligations and complying with its NDA obligations. The exclusion should be conditioned on the Receiving Party providing prompt notice to the Disclosing Party (to the extent legally permissible) and reasonably cooperating with any effort by the Disclosing Party to obtain a protective order or other appropriate remedy.

**Fallback Position.** Not applicable. All five exclusions are required in every M&amp;A NDA to which Verdantis is a party. There is no fallback position that would permit the omission of any exclusion.

**Walk-Away Position.** The omission of any of the five standard exclusions listed above constitutes a walk-away position. The absence of any exclusion — and particularly the absence of the independent-development exclusion — is classified as a **High Risk** deviation requiring approval from both the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) and the VP, Corporate Development (Prescott) before Verdantis may agree to execute the NDA.

**Negotiation Note.** Counterparty drafts commonly omit the independent-development exclusion, particularly when the counterparty is a private company or a company with less sophisticated M&amp;A counsel. In some cases, counterparties may also attempt to modify the prior-possession or third-party-receipt exclusions by removing the knowledge qualifier or adding onerous evidentiary requirements (such as requiring "clear and convincing evidence" rather than contemporaneous written records). These modifications should be resisted.

Outside counsel must redline any counterparty draft that omits the independent-development exclusion to insert the full exclusion and should explain to counterparty counsel that this exclusion is a market-standard protection routinely included in technology M&amp;A NDAs and that it protects both parties equally. If the counterparty persists in refusing to include the independent-development exclusion, outside counsel must escalate the issue to the Verdantis legal team for High Risk approval before agreeing to proceed without it.

**Section 3.2 — Definition of "Representatives" / "Permitted Disclosees"**

**Preferred Position.** The term "Representatives" (or "Permitted Disclosees," depending on the counterparty's preferred nomenclature) must be defined broadly to include the Receiving Party's directors, officers, and employees, together with the following categories of external advisors and parties, in each case who have a need to know the Confidential Information in connection with evaluating or consummating the Transaction:

> • Legal counsel (including outside counsel retained by the Receiving Party for the Transaction or for related legal matters);
>
> • Financial advisors (including investment bankers, valuation consultants, and other financial advisors engaged by the Receiving Party);
>
> • Accounting advisors and auditors (including the Receiving Party's independent auditors and any accounting advisors specifically engaged for the Transaction);
>
> • Potential lenders and their respective advisors (including banks, financial institutions, and other credit providers that the Receiving Party may approach in connection with financing the Transaction, together with the legal, financial, and accounting advisors of such potential lenders);
>
> • Potential co-investors and their respective advisors (including equity co-investors, co-bidders, consortium members, and similar parties, together with their legal, financial, and accounting advisors).

The definition must be sufficiently broad to encompass Verdantis's existing advisor relationships and to ensure that Verdantis can share Confidential Information with the full range of professional advisors necessary to evaluate and, if applicable, consummate a Transaction. Verdantis's current advisor relationships include, among others, its outside counsel, Thorncastle Whitfield LLP (which serves as Verdantis's primary outside counsel for NDA-phase engagements and early-stage M&amp;A matters), and its financial advisors, Ridgeline Partners LLC (which serves as Verdantis's retained financial advisor for strategic transactions). Any definition of "Representatives" that would exclude either of these advisor categories — or their functional equivalents — is unacceptable.

**Fallback Position.** If the counterparty insists on limiting the definition to an enumerated list of named categories rather than a general, open-ended formulation (e.g., "advisors and representatives who have a need to know"), Verdantis will accept a closed list provided that every category identified under the Preferred Position above is expressly included in the list. In this scenario, Verdantis may also agree to a requirement that external advisors (i.e., advisors who are not directors, officers, or employees of the Receiving Party) be bound by written confidentiality obligations no less restrictive than those set forth in the NDA, or that such advisors be bound by professional ethical obligations of confidentiality (as is the case for legal counsel and, in many jurisdictions, accounting professionals). Verdantis should resist any requirement that external advisors execute a separate NDA or joinder agreement, as this imposes unnecessary administrative burden and may delay the Transaction timeline.

**Walk-Away Position.** A definition of "Representatives" or "Permitted Disclosees" that is limited to the Receiving Party's directors, officers, and employees only — and that excludes external legal counsel, financial advisors, potential lenders, and potential co-investors — constitutes a **High Risk** deviation and is unacceptable without approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) and the VP, Corporate Development (Prescott). Such a restriction would functionally prevent Verdantis from sharing Confidential Information with its transaction advisors and would render the NDA operationally untenable, as it would preclude Verdantis from obtaining the legal, financial, and strategic advice necessary to evaluate the Transaction. Outside counsel should redline to expand the definition to include all of the Preferred Position categories and should explain to counterparty counsel that a restriction of this nature is inconsistent with market practice for M&amp;A NDAs involving public companies and would impede Verdantis's ability to conduct meaningful diligence.

**Section 3.3 — Scope and Form of Confidential Information**

**Preferred Position.** The definition of "Confidential Information" should encompass information disclosed in any form, including written, oral, electronic, and visual. The definition should be broad enough to capture technical data and specifications, financial information and projections, operational data and metrics, commercial terms and customer information, strategic plans and analyses, intellectual property and trade secrets, and personnel information, in each case to the extent disclosed by the Disclosing Party or its Representatives in connection with the Transaction. The definition should also cover the fact that discussions or negotiations regarding the Transaction are occurring, the terms and conditions of those discussions, and the existence or content of any proposals or offers exchanged between the parties, in each case subject to the securities-law carve-out described in Section 6.1 of this playbook.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a reasonable narrowing of the scope of the definition to information that is marked or designated as "Confidential" (or words of similar import) at the time of disclosure, or within a specified time period following disclosure, provided that orally disclosed information is covered if the Disclosing Party provides written confirmation of the confidential nature of such information within 10 business days following the date of oral disclosure. This marking/designation requirement is a common feature of NDAs in commercial contexts and is acceptable provided that the 10-business-day confirmation period for oral disclosures is included, as the rapid pace of M&amp;A diligence makes it impractical to mark every oral communication in real time.

**Walk-Away Position.** A limitation of the definition of "Confidential Information" to written materials only — excluding oral, electronic, and visual disclosures — is unacceptable. The nature of M&amp;A due diligence necessarily involves extensive oral communications, management presentations, facility tours, and technology demonstrations, all of which may involve the disclosure of highly sensitive information. An NDA that fails to cover oral and visual disclosures would leave Verdantis without meaningful protection for a significant portion of the information exchanged during the diligence process.

**Section 3.4 — Mutual vs. Unilateral Scope**

**Preferred Position.** Verdantis's strong preference is for a mutual NDA under which both parties simultaneously function as Disclosing Party and Receiving Party. A mutual structure ensures reciprocity of obligations and protections and is appropriate for the vast majority of M&amp;A contexts, where both parties will exchange confidential information during the course of discussions and diligence.

**Fallback Position.** A unilateral NDA is acceptable where Verdantis is the Receiving Party only (i.e., where Verdantis is the potential acquiror and the counterparty is the potential target, and only the target is disclosing confidential information). In this scenario, the scope of Verdantis's obligations as Receiving Party must be otherwise consistent with the positions set forth in this playbook. Outside counsel should ensure that the unilateral structure does not impose disproportionate obligations on Verdantis as compared to a mutual NDA.

**Walk-Away Position.** A unilateral NDA under which Verdantis is the Disclosing Party only, with no reciprocal confidentiality protection for information that the counterparty may disclose to Verdantis, is unacceptable. Even in situations where Verdantis expects to be the primary discloser of confidential information, a unilateral structure that provides no reciprocal protection creates an asymmetry of obligations that is inconsistent with Verdantis's standard negotiation posture. Outside counsel should counter any unilateral counterparty draft with Verdantis's mutual form NDA.

**Section 3.5 — Warranty Disclaimer and Accuracy of Information**

**Preferred Position.** Verdantis accepts and, in many cases, will affirmatively propose the inclusion of a provision disclaiming warranties as to the accuracy, completeness, or fitness for a particular purpose of the Confidential Information. Warranty disclaimers are market-standard in M&amp;A NDAs and serve the important function of ensuring that the NDA is not construed as creating representations or warranties regarding the quality or reliability of diligence materials, which are properly addressed in the definitive transaction agreement (if any). However, any such disclaimer must include a savings clause expressly preserving claims for **fraud or intentional misrepresentation**. The purpose of the savings clause is to ensure that the warranty disclaimer cannot be invoked as a shield by a party that affirmatively and intentionally makes false or misleading disclosures of material information.

Suggested language for the savings clause:

> *"Notwithstanding the foregoing, nothing in this Section shall limit or otherwise affect the liability of either party for fraud or intentional misrepresentation in connection with the disclosure of Confidential Information."*

**Fallback Position.** If the counterparty resists the inclusion of an express fraud/intentional-misrepresentation savings clause — for example, on the ground that such a clause is unnecessary because fraud claims are not waivable as a matter of law — outside counsel should counter with alternative language providing that the warranty disclaimer does not apply to any representation or disclosure made by a party "with actual knowledge of its falsity." This formulation achieves substantially the same result as the preferred savings clause while framing the issue as a knowledge-based limitation rather than an affirmative preservation of fraud claims.

**Walk-Away Position.** A blanket warranty disclaimer with no savings clause that purports to eliminate claims for fraud or intentional misrepresentation is classified as a **Low Risk** deviation if limited to the warranty-disclaimer section of the NDA. However, the deviation becomes a **Medium Risk** deviation if the disclaimer purports to apply to the NDA as a whole or to limit or cap the remedies available for breach of the NDA's confidentiality obligations. Outside counsel should not agree to any formulation — regardless of its placement in the NDA — that effectively immunizes a party against liability for fraud or intentional misrepresentation. If the counterparty insists on a broad, unqualified disclaimer, outside counsel should escalate the issue to the Verdantis legal team for classification and approval.

**Section 4 — Duration and Survival**

The provisions governing the duration of the NDA's term, the survival of confidentiality obligations, and the treatment of information retained in unaided memory (the residuals clause) are among the most frequently negotiated provisions in M&amp;A NDAs. Verdantis's positions on these topics reflect a careful balance between the need to protect confidential information for a meaningful period and the operational reality that excessively long or indefinite obligations create compliance burdens and legal uncertainty.

**Section 4.1 — Survival of Confidentiality Obligations**

**Preferred Position.** Confidentiality obligations under the NDA shall survive for a period of **2 years** from the date of execution of the NDA, regardless of whether a Transaction is consummated and regardless of whether the NDA is terminated earlier by either party. This 2-year survival period represents Verdantis's assessment of the period during which confidential information disclosed in an M&amp;A context retains material commercial sensitivity, taking into account the pace of change in the enterprise software and data analytics industries in which Verdantis operates. At the expiration of the survival period, the confidentiality obligations cease and the former Receiving Party is free to use or disclose the previously confidential information without restriction (subject to any obligations arising independently under applicable trade secret law or other legal doctrines).

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a survival period of up to **3 years** from the date of execution of the NDA. A survival period between 2 and 3 years is classified as a **Medium Risk** deviation and requires approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy). When seeking such approval, outside counsel should include in the deviation memorandum an assessment of the commercial sensitivity and expected shelf life of the information likely to be disclosed, as well as the counterparty's stated rationale for requiring the longer period.

**Walk-Away Position.** Any survival period exceeding **3 years** is unacceptable and must be rejected. Outside counsel should redline any survival period in excess of 3 years to reduce it to the 2-year preferred position and should explain to counterparty counsel that Verdantis's internal policy establishes 3 years as the maximum permissible exposure period for confidentiality obligations in an M&amp;A NDA context. If the counterparty insists on a survival period in excess of 3 years and is unwilling to negotiate, outside counsel must escalate the issue as follows: a counterparty demand for a survival period between 3 and 5 years that is the subject of active negotiation remains a **Medium Risk** deviation; however, a counterparty's firm insistence on a survival period exceeding 3 years with no willingness to negotiate becomes a **High Risk**deviation requiring approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) and the VP, Corporate Development (Prescott).

**Negotiation Note.** Counterparty drafts frequently propose 5-year survival periods, and it is not uncommon — particularly in deals involving private-equity sponsors or counterparties in industries with long product development cycles (e.g., pharmaceuticals, defense contracting) — to encounter proposals for indefinite survival (i.e., "the obligations hereunder shall survive in perpetuity"). Indefinite survival provisions are particularly disfavored by Verdantis because they create open-ended compliance obligations that are difficult to administer and monitor over time. Outside counsel should treat any indefinite survival proposal as presumptively requiring reduction to 2 years and should escalate in accordance with the deviation approval procedures if the counterparty resists.

**Section 4.2 — Term and Termination of the NDA**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA shall have a defined term of **12 months** from the date of execution, during which period the parties may exchange Confidential Information in connection with evaluating and, if applicable, consummating the Transaction. The NDA may also include a provision permitting either party to terminate the NDA at any time upon written notice to the other party. In either case — whether the NDA expires at the end of its stated term or is terminated earlier — the confidentiality obligations set forth in the NDA shall survive termination or expiration in accordance with the survival period specified in Section 4.1.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a term of up to **18 months** from the date of execution, particularly in complex transactions where the evaluation period is expected to be lengthy. A term of between 12 and 18 months is a **Low Risk** deviation and may be approved by the Associate General Counsel (Castellano).

**Walk-Away Position.** An open-ended or "evergreen" term — i.e., a term that continues indefinitely with no expiration date and no termination mechanism — is unacceptable. Evergreen NDAs create indefinite information-exchange periods and make it difficult to establish a clear point at which the return/destruction obligations are triggered. Outside counsel should redline any evergreen term to include a defined expiration date and a mutual right of termination upon written notice.

**Section 4.3 — Residuals Clause**

**Preferred Position (Mandatory).** The NDA must include a **residuals clause** permitting each party's personnel to use general knowledge, ideas, concepts, know-how, and techniques that are retained in the unaided memory of the Receiving Party's Representatives following their exposure to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information, provided that such use does not constitute disclosure of specific Confidential Information or the reproduction of any tangible embodiment of Confidential Information. The residuals clause is a mandatory provision and must appear in every M&amp;A NDA to which Verdantis is a party.

The inclusion of a residuals clause is essential given the nature of Verdantis's business. Verdantis is an enterprise software and data analytics company whose personnel — including engineers, data scientists, product managers, and business development professionals — routinely acquire general knowledge and insights through their professional activities. In the course of evaluating a potential Transaction, these personnel may be exposed to the counterparty's technology, business processes, and market strategies. Without a residuals clause, the mere fact that Verdantis personnel who participated in due diligence subsequently work on related projects or products could give rise to claims that Verdantis misappropriated the counterparty's Confidential Information, even if the personnel in question relied solely on their own general knowledge and expertise.

Suggested language for the residuals clause:

> *"Nothing in this Agreement shall restrict the right of either party to use Residual Information for any purpose. 'Residual Information' means ideas, concepts, know-how, or techniques relating to the Disclosing Party's technology or business that are retained in the unaided memory of Representatives of the Receiving Party who had access to Confidential Information, without reference to any written or other tangible form of such Confidential Information. The Receiving Party shall have no obligation to limit or restrict the scope of work or reassignment of its Representatives who have had access to Confidential Information, provided that the Receiving Party shall not intentionally cause or direct any such Representative to memorize Confidential Information for the purpose of circumventing the restrictions set forth in this Agreement."*

**Fallback Position.** Not applicable. A residuals clause is mandatory and must be included in every M&amp;A NDA. If the counterparty's draft omits a residuals clause, outside counsel must redline the draft to insert the residuals clause language set forth above (or substantially equivalent language) and must explain to counterparty counsel that this provision is a non-negotiable requirement for Verdantis.

**Walk-Away Position.** The omission of a residuals clause from the NDA is classified as a **Medium Risk** deviation requiring approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy). Given the nature of Verdantis's business, the absence of a residuals clause creates material risk that routine subsequent product development, engineering, and business development activities by Verdantis personnel who participated in the due diligence process could be characterized as misappropriation of the counterparty's Confidential Information. This risk is particularly acute in situations where the counterparty operates in a market adjacent to Verdantis's existing products or services, as there is an increased likelihood that Verdantis personnel will work on related projects following the conclusion of discussions.

**Negotiation Note.** Counterparties in data-intensive industries — particularly those with significant proprietary datasets, algorithms, or machine-learning models — frequently resist the inclusion of a residuals clause on the ground that it effectively permits the Receiving Party to reverse-engineer or replicate the Disclosing Party's proprietary technology through memorization. Outside counsel should address this concern by emphasizing the following points: (i) the residuals clause protects both parties equally and is bilateral in a mutual NDA; (ii) the clause is limited to information retained in "unaided memory" and does not authorize the use of any written, electronic, or tangible embodiment of Confidential Information; (iii) the clause includes a prohibition on intentional memorization for the purpose of circumventing the NDA; and (iv) residuals clauses are market-standard in technology M&amp;A transactions and are routinely included in NDAs negotiated by sophisticated parties in the enterprise software sector.

**Section 5 — Return and Destruction of Confidential Information**

The return and destruction provisions of the NDA govern the disposition of Confidential Information following the conclusion of discussions regarding the Transaction. These provisions must strike a balance between the Disclosing Party's legitimate interest in controlling the dissemination and retention of its Confidential Information and the Receiving Party's operational reality — particularly as a publicly traded company subject to regulatory record-retention requirements and operating complex enterprise information-technology systems.

**Section 5.1 — Triggering Events**

**Preferred Position.** The obligation to return or destroy Confidential Information shall arise upon the earliest of: (a) receipt by the Receiving Party of a written request from the Disclosing Party at any time (whether during or after the term of the NDA); or (b) termination or expiration of discussions regarding the Transaction, whether such termination occurs by mutual agreement, by unilateral decision of either party, or by operation of the NDA's term. The triggering-event provision should be drafted to provide clarity as to the commencement of the return/destruction timeline and should avoid ambiguous formulations that could give rise to disputes about when the obligation was triggered.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a provision under which the obligation to return or destroy Confidential Information is triggered only upon written request by the Disclosing Party, without an automatic trigger upon termination of discussions. This formulation gives both parties greater flexibility and avoids the practical difficulty of determining the precise date on which discussions were "terminated" in situations where discussions wind down gradually rather than ending at a defined point. This is a **Low Risk** deviation.

**Walk-Away Position.** An NDA that contains no return or destruction obligation whatsoever — i.e., that permits the Receiving Party to retain all Confidential Information indefinitely without any obligation to return or destroy it upon request or upon termination of discussions — is unacceptable. Outside counsel should redline to include at minimum the fallback position (return/destruction upon written request).

**Section 5.2 — Timeline and Carve-Outs**

**Preferred Position.** Upon the occurrence of a triggering event described in Section 5.1, the Receiving Party shall return to the Disclosing Party or destroy (at the Receiving Party's election) all Confidential Information, including all copies, notes, summaries, analyses, compilations, and other materials prepared by the Receiving Party or its Representatives that contain, reflect, or are derived from Confidential Information, within **15 business days** of the triggering event. Upon completion of such return or destruction, the Receiving Party shall provide a written certification of destruction signed by an authorized officer of the Receiving Party, in accordance with Section 5.3.

The return/destruction obligation must be subject to the following two carve-outs, both of which are **required** in every M&amp;A NDA to which Verdantis is a party:

> 1. **Regulatory/Legal Holds.** The Receiving Party may retain copies of Confidential Information to the extent required by applicable law, regulation, or bona fide internal document-retention policy, or to the extent subject to a litigation hold, regulatory investigation, or other preservation obligation imposed by a court, regulatory authority, or the Receiving Party's legal counsel. Such retained copies (collectively, "Retained Copies") remain subject to the confidentiality obligations of the NDA for the full duration of the applicable survival period set forth in Section 4.1. This carve-out is essential for Verdantis as a publicly traded company subject to SEC record-retention requirements, Nasdaq rules, and potential litigation holds arising from shareholder or regulatory matters.
>
> 2. **Electronically Archived Copies.** The Receiving Party shall not be required to purge, delete, or otherwise remove Confidential Information from electronic archival, backup, or disaster-recovery systems (including, without limitation, disaster-recovery tapes, automated email archive systems, network backup servers, and similar systems) where deletion is not commercially practicable using reasonable efforts. This carve-out recognizes the technological reality that modern enterprise information systems maintain automated backups and archives from which selective deletion of specific information is often technically infeasible or prohibitively expensive. Confidential Information retained on such systems shall not be intentionally accessed by the Receiving Party or its Representatives following the return/destruction date and shall remain subject to the NDA's confidentiality obligations for the full duration of the applicable survival period.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a return/destruction timeline of no fewer than **10 business days** following the triggering event, provided that both of the carve-outs described above (regulatory/legal holds and electronically archived copies) are retained in the NDA. A timeline between 10 and 15 business days is a **Low Risk** deviation if both carve-outs are preserved. A timeline of fewer than 15 business days without one or both of the carve-outs is a **Medium Risk** deviation.

**Walk-Away Position.** A return/destruction deadline of fewer than **10 business days** without the regulatory/legal-hold and electronic-archive carve-outs is classified as a **Medium Risk** deviation requiring approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy). Deadlines of 5 business days or fewer without carve-outs are operationally infeasible for a publicly traded company of Verdantis's size and organizational complexity (approximately 3,200 employees across 12 offices) and should be redlined to the 15-business-day preferred position. Outside counsel should explain to counterparty counsel that the compressed timeline and absence of carve-outs would require Verdantis to complete a comprehensive data-collection and destruction process across multiple business units, information systems, and geographic locations in a timeframe that is practically impossible to achieve with reasonable certainty of compliance.

**Section 5.3 — Destruction Certification**

**Preferred Position.** Upon completion of the destruction of Confidential Information (other than Retained Copies permitted under the regulatory/legal-hold and electronic-archive carve-outs), the Receiving Party shall provide a written certification to the Disclosing Party confirming that all Confidential Information (other than Retained Copies) has been destroyed. The certification shall be signed by an authorized officer of the Receiving Party and shall be delivered within the return/destruction timeline specified in Section 5.2.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a provision under which the destruction certification is signed by in-house counsel of the Receiving Party rather than by an officer. This is a **Low Risk** deviation. Outside counsel should ensure that the in-house counsel providing the certification is authorized to do so on behalf of the Receiving Party and that the certification obligation is not unduly qualified (e.g., "to the best of counsel's knowledge" qualifications are acceptable; qualifications that effectively render the certification non-binding are not).

**Walk-Away Position.** An NDA that contains no certification requirement — i.e., that imposes a destruction obligation but does not require the Receiving Party to confirm compliance — is a **Low Risk** deviation. While a certification requirement is preferred, its absence does not, standing alone, create significant legal exposure, as the underlying destruction obligation remains enforceable regardless of whether a certification is provided.

**Section 6 — Restrictions on Disclosure and Use**

This Section addresses the affirmative obligations and restrictions imposed on the Receiving Party with respect to the disclosure, use, and treatment of Confidential Information, as well as related restrictions on the solicitation of the Disclosing Party's personnel.

**Section 6.1 — Non-Disclosure of Discussions / Existence of Transaction**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA shall include a provision restricting each party from disclosing to any third party the existence of the NDA, the fact that discussions or negotiations regarding the Transaction are occurring or have occurred, the terms or conditions of the Transaction under discussion, or the existence or content of any proposals, offers, or counteroffers exchanged between the parties (collectively, "Transaction Information"). This non-disclosure-of-discussions provision is a standard feature of M&amp;A NDAs and serves the important commercial function of preventing premature market speculation, maintaining the parties' negotiating leverage, and avoiding potential disruption to the business operations, employee relations, and market position of either party.

However, the non-disclosure-of-discussions provision must contain a **mandatory carve-out** permitting disclosures of Transaction Information required under:

> • **Applicable securities laws**, including without limitation the Securities Act of 1933, as amended, the Securities Exchange Act of 1934, as amended, and the rules and regulations promulgated thereunder by the United States Securities and Exchange Commission (the "SEC"), including Regulation FD;
>
> • **Stock-exchange rules**, including the rules and listing standards of the Nasdaq Stock Market LLC, given Verdantis's listing on Nasdaq under the ticker symbol VRDS;
>
> • **Subpoena, court order, or other compulsory legal process**, including civil or criminal subpoenas, regulatory demands, and orders or requests issued by any court, governmental authority, or self-regulatory organization.

This carve-out is essential given Verdantis's status as a publicly traded company. Verdantis is subject to continuous disclosure obligations under the federal securities laws, including the obligation to file current reports on Form 8-K upon the occurrence of specified events, to disclose material information in periodic reports, and to comply with Regulation FD's prohibition on selective disclosure of material nonpublic information. Failure to include this carve-out could place Verdantis in the untenable and legally indefensible position of having to choose between compliance with its contractual obligations under the NDA and compliance with its mandatory disclosure obligations under the federal securities laws and Nasdaq listing rules. In the event of any conflict between the NDA obligations and securities-law disclosure requirements, the securities-law obligations must control.

**Fallback Position.** If the counterparty resists the inclusion of the full carve-out as described above, the minimum acceptable formulation must include, at a minimum, carve-outs for applicable securities laws and stock-exchange rules. A carve-out limited to court orders or compulsory legal process alone is insufficient because it does not address the affirmative disclosure obligations imposed on public companies by the securities laws and exchange rules, which may require disclosure even in the absence of a court order or subpoena. Outside counsel should explain that this carve-out protects the counterparty as well, to the extent the counterparty is or may become a publicly traded company during the term of the NDA.

**Walk-Away Position.** The omission of any securities-law carve-out in the non-disclosure-of-discussions provision is a **High Risk** deviation requiring approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) and the VP, Corporate Development (Prescott). Outside counsel must flag this issue as a compliance-critical concern in the Issues Memorandum and must clearly communicate to the Verdantis deal team and the approving authorities that execution of an NDA without this carve-out could expose Verdantis to conflicting legal obligations with potentially material consequences, including SEC enforcement action, Nasdaq delisting proceedings, or shareholder litigation.

**Section 6.2 — Use Limitation**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA shall provide that Confidential Information received by the Receiving Party may be used solely for the purpose of evaluating and, if applicable, negotiating and consummating the Transaction. No other use of the Confidential Information is permitted, including without limitation use for competitive purposes, product development unrelated to the Transaction, or any commercial exploitation of the Confidential Information. This use limitation is a fundamental component of the NDA and is not subject to negotiation.

**Fallback Position.** None. The use-limitation provision described above is a standard, market-accepted formulation and does not require a fallback position. Outside counsel should not propose or accept any modifications to the use limitation that would expand the permitted uses of Confidential Information beyond evaluation and consummation of the Transaction.

**Walk-Away Position.** An NDA that permits the Receiving Party to use Confidential Information for purposes other than the evaluation and consummation of the Transaction — including, without limitation, for the Receiving Party's general business purposes, competitive intelligence, or product development — is unacceptable. Outside counsel should redline to restore the standard use limitation and should escalate to the Verdantis legal team if the counterparty resists.

**Section 6.3 — Non-Solicitation of Personnel**

**Preferred Position.** Verdantis's preferred position is to include **no non-solicitation provision** in the M&amp;A NDA. Non-solicitation provisions restrict Verdantis's ability to recruit talented personnel and may create unnecessary friction in the negotiation of the NDA, particularly where the counterparty operates in a market in which Verdantis routinely recruits. In many cases, the inclusion of a non-solicitation provision is not warranted by the nature or stage of the Transaction, and outside counsel should resist its inclusion in the first instance.

If the counterparty requires the inclusion of a non-solicitation provision as a condition to proceeding with discussions, Verdantis will accept such a provision only if it conforms to the following parameters:

> **Duration.** The non-solicitation restriction shall have a maximum duration of **12 months** from the date of the NDA. Verdantis will not agree to a non-solicitation period exceeding 12 months.
>
> **Scope.** The non-solicitation restriction shall be limited to personnel of the Disclosing Party who are **introduced to the Receiving Party in connection with the due diligence process** for the Transaction. The non-solicitation must not extend to personnel who are merely "contacted" during the due diligence process or whose names appear in documents, organizational charts, or other materials reviewed by the Receiving Party during diligence. The distinction between "introduced" and "contacted" is critical: the purpose of the non-solicitation provision is to prevent the Receiving Party from exploiting relationships formed through the diligence process, not to create a blanket prohibition on hiring the Disclosing Party's entire workforce based on incidental contact or document review.
>
> **Exceptions.** The non-solicitation provision must include the following standard carve-outs:
>
> • **General solicitations.** The non-solicitation shall not apply to solicitations resulting from general recruitment efforts, including job postings on the Receiving Party's website or third-party job boards, print or online advertisements of general circulation, and engagements of executive search firms, in each case that are not specifically directed at the Disclosing Party's personnel.
>
> • **Unsolicited contacts.** The non-solicitation shall not apply to personnel of the Disclosing Party who initiate contact with the Receiving Party or apply for employment with the Receiving Party without any solicitation by the Receiving Party.
>
> **Remedy.** Any non-solicitation provision must be subject to an **actual-damages standard only**. Verdantis will not agree to **liquidated-damages provisions** for non-solicitation breaches. Liquidated damages in the non-solicitation context are difficult to justify as reasonable pre-estimates of harm, as the actual damage caused by the solicitation or hiring of a single employee varies enormously depending on the employee's role, seniority, and replaceability. Moreover, liquidated-damages provisions for non-solicitation breaches may be vulnerable to challenge under Delaware law as unenforceable penalties, particularly where the stipulated damages bear no reasonable relationship to the anticipated or actual harm. Outside counsel should redline to delete any liquidated-damages provision for non-solicitation breaches and replace it with a general remedies clause referencing actual damages and, where appropriate, equitable relief.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a non-solicitation provision with a duration of up to 12 months and scope limited to personnel introduced in connection with the due diligence process. In addition, Verdantis may accept a provision that restricts the Receiving Party from *hiring* (as opposed to merely *soliciting*) introduced personnel for up to 12 months, provided that the general-solicitation and unsolicited-contact exceptions described above are included. A "no-hire" provision is broader than a "no-solicit" provision because it restricts the Receiving Party from employing the relevant personnel regardless of who initiated the contact; however, a 12-month no-hire provision with appropriate exceptions is within the range of acceptable terms. In all cases, the actual-damages standard must be preserved and liquidated damages must not be accepted.

**Walk-Away Position.** The following are classified as **High Risk** deviations, each requiring approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) and the VP, Corporate Development (Prescott):

> • A non-solicitation (or no-hire) period exceeding **12 months** in duration;
>
> • A non-solicitation scope that extends to any employee "contacted" during the due diligence process (as opposed to those "introduced" in connection with the process);
>
> • Any provision imposing **liquidated damages** for non-solicitation breaches, regardless of the amount of the stipulated damages.

Outside counsel must redline any counterparty draft containing these provisions to conform to the Preferred Position and must explain to counterparty counsel that each of these terms falls outside Verdantis's acceptable range. If the counterparty is unwilling to modify its position on any of these terms, outside counsel must escalate to the Verdantis legal team for High Risk approval before proceeding.

**Section 7 — Standstill Provisions**

Standstill provisions restrict the Receiving Party's ability to acquire voting securities of the Disclosing Party, make unsolicited acquisition proposals, or take other actions designed to effect a change of control of the Disclosing Party. Standstill provisions are the most strategically sensitive provisions in an M&amp;A NDA and receive the highest level of internal scrutiny at Verdantis. The acceptance of any standstill provision — regardless of duration, scope, or the inclusion of mitigating terms — triggers the board notification protocol described in Section 7.2.

**Section 7.1 — Standstill**

**Preferred Position.** Verdantis's strong and unequivocal preference is to accept **no standstill provision** in any M&amp;A NDA. Standstill provisions restrict Verdantis's strategic flexibility and may impede its ability to pursue value-maximizing transactions for its shareholders. In particular, a standstill may prevent Verdantis from:

> • Making a competing bid for the counterparty if the counterparty subsequently enters into discussions with a third party on more favorable terms;
>
> • Acquiring voting securities of the counterparty on the open market at an attractive price;
>
> • Publicly communicating its interest in a transaction with the counterparty in circumstances where public communication may advance shareholder value; and
>
> • Exercising other strategic options that may become available during the standstill period.

For these reasons, outside counsel should resist the inclusion of any standstill provision in the first instance and should explain to counterparty counsel that Verdantis's standard M&amp;A NDA does not include a standstill.

**Fallback Position.** If a standstill provision is commercially necessary to secure access to the counterparty's data room or to advance discussions to a stage where a transaction can be meaningfully evaluated, Verdantis may accept a standstill provision subject to **all** of the following conditions:

> **Duration.** The standstill shall have a maximum duration of **12 months** from the date of execution of the NDA. Verdantis will not accept a standstill period exceeding 12 months under any circumstances.
>
> **Mandatory Fall-Away.** The standstill shall automatically terminate, without notice or further action by either party, upon the earlier of:
>
> • The counterparty entering into a **definitive agreement** with any third party for a transaction involving the acquisition of a controlling interest in the counterparty (whether by merger, tender offer, asset acquisition, or otherwise); or
>
> • The counterparty commencing a **public auction process**, including without limitation by filing a proxy statement, issuing a press release, making a public announcement that it is exploring strategic alternatives or soliciting competing proposals, or otherwise publicly inviting third-party interest in acquiring the counterparty.
>
> The mandatory fall-away provision is essential to prevent the counterparty from using the standstill to "lock up" Verdantis while simultaneously soliciting competing offers from other parties. Without a fall-away provision, the counterparty could exploit the standstill to reduce competitive pressure and potentially extract a higher price from a third-party acquiror, to the detriment of Verdantis's strategic interests.
>
> **Scope.** The standstill shall prohibit only the following actions: (i) acquiring, agreeing to acquire, or offering to acquire beneficial ownership of voting securities of the counterparty (other than through passive market purchases of less than a specified threshold); and (ii) making or publicly announcing any unsolicited proposal or offer for a merger, acquisition, business combination, or similar transaction involving the counterparty. The standstill shall **not** restrict Verdantis from privately communicating with the counterparty's Board of Directors or senior management regarding a potential transaction, including making private proposals, requesting meetings, or expressing interest in a transaction, provided that such communications are not publicly disclosed by Verdantis.

**Walk-Away Position.** Acceptance of **any** standstill provision — even one conforming to all of the fallback parameters described above — constitutes a **High Risk** deviation and requires approval from both the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) and the VP, Corporate Development (Prescott). In addition, the acceptance of any standstill provision triggers the **board-level notification** requirement described in Section 7.2 below.

The following are absolute walk-away positions that Verdantis will not accept under any circumstances, regardless of the commercial context:

> • A standstill period exceeding **12 months** in duration;
>
> • A standstill without a **mandatory fall-away provision** of the type described above;
>
> • A standstill that restricts Verdantis from **privately communicating** with the counterparty's Board of Directors regarding a potential transaction;
>
> • **"Don't ask, don't waive"** provisions — i.e., provisions that prohibit Verdantis from requesting a waiver or modification of the standstill and/or prohibit the counterparty's Board of Directors from granting such a waiver, even if the Board determines that doing so would be in the best interests of the counterparty's shareholders. "Don't ask, don't waive" provisions are disfavored by the Delaware Court of Chancery and are inconsistent with the fiduciary obligations of the counterparty's Board of Directors.

If a counterparty insists on any of the absolute walk-away terms described above, outside counsel must inform the counterparty that the term is outside Verdantis's acceptable range and escalate the matter to the Verdantis legal team. Outside counsel should not communicate Verdantis's willingness to terminate negotiations over the standstill provision without first confirming with the deal team that such a communication is authorized.

**Section 7.2 — Board Notification Protocol for Standstill Acceptance**

Whenever outside counsel or the deal team identifies that a standstill provision will be accepted — even one conforming to all of the fallback parameters described in Section 7.1 — the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) must prepare a memorandum to the Board of Directors of Verdantis Solutions Inc. summarizing the following:

> (i) The terms of the standstill provision, including its duration, scope, and fall-away triggers;
>
> (ii) The commercial rationale for accepting the standstill, including the strategic importance of the Transaction and the information access that the standstill is expected to unlock;
>
> (iii) The fall-away triggers and the circumstances under which the standstill would automatically terminate;
>
> (iv) The impact of the standstill on Verdantis's strategic alternatives, including any limitations on Verdantis's ability to pursue other acquisition opportunities or to acquire voting securities of the counterparty during the standstill period; and
>
> (v) Any mitigating terms or concessions obtained from the counterparty in exchange for Verdantis's acceptance of the standstill.

The board notification memorandum shall be prepared by the General Counsel and delivered to the Board through the Corporate Secretary. Board notification must occur **before** Verdantis executes the NDA containing the standstill provision. The General Counsel shall coordinate with the Corporate Secretary to determine the appropriate method of board notification (e.g., written consent, special meeting, or discussion at the next regularly scheduled board meeting), taking into account the urgency of the Transaction timeline and the availability of Board members.

The board notification requirement is a notification obligation, not an approval requirement. The Board is not required to approve the standstill provision; rather, the purpose of the notification is to ensure that the Board is informed of any standstill obligations that may affect Verdantis's strategic flexibility and to provide the Board with an opportunity to raise concerns or provide guidance. However, if any Board member objects to the acceptance of the standstill, the General Counsel and VP, Corporate Development must consult with the Board (or the relevant committee of the Board) before proceeding with execution of the NDA.

**Section 8 — Remedies**

The remedies provisions of the NDA determine the universe of relief available to a non-breaching party in the event of a breach of the NDA's confidentiality or other obligations. Verdantis's positions on remedies are designed to preserve the full range of legal and equitable relief while resisting provisions that either limit available remedies or create one-sided procedural advantages for the breaching party.

**Section 8.1 — General Remedies**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA should provide that a breach of any provision of the NDA entitles the non-breaching party to seek all remedies available at law or in equity, including without limitation compensatory damages, consequential damages (to the extent proven), specific performance, and injunctive relief. The remedies clause should not include any limitation on the types of damages available, any cap on liability, or any exclusion of consequential, incidental, or indirect damages.

**Fallback Position.** Same as the Preferred Position. Verdantis's position on the availability of the full range of remedies is not subject to negotiation. A general remedies clause preserving all available legal and equitable relief is a market-standard provision in M&amp;A NDAs and should not be controversial.

**Walk-Away Position.** Any provision that limits the remedies available for breach of the NDA — including any cap on liability, any exclusion of consequential or indirect damages, or any limitation on the types of claims that may be asserted — is unacceptable. Outside counsel should redline to delete any limitation-of-remedies or limitation-of-liability provision and should explain to counterparty counsel that such provisions are inconsistent with market practice for M&amp;A NDAs, where the potential harm from a breach of confidentiality is often difficult to quantify and may manifest in indirect or consequential forms (e.g., loss of competitive advantage, decline in stock price, damage to business relationships).

**Section 8.2 — Equitable Relief / Injunctive Relief**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA may include a provision acknowledging that a breach of the confidentiality obligations set forth in the NDA could cause **irreparable harm** to the Disclosing Party and that **equitable relief, including injunctive relief,** may be an appropriate remedy in addition to all other remedies available at law or in equity. Such an acknowledgment is common in M&amp;A NDAs and serves to support the Disclosing Party's ability to obtain expedited injunctive relief in the event of a breach.

However, Verdantis requires that the following two limitations be observed in any equitable-relief provision:

> **No stipulated findings of irreparable harm.** The NDA must **require a court determination** that irreparable harm exists before injunctive relief may issue. Provisions in which the parties "agree," "stipulate," "acknowledge," or "consent" that a breach of the NDA will constitute irreparable harm as a matter of law — and that the non-breaching party is therefore entitled to injunctive relief without the need to demonstrate irreparable harm to the satisfaction of the court — are not acceptable. Stipulated findings of irreparable harm effectively waive one of the fundamental elements of the test for injunctive relief and deprive the restrained party of the ability to contest the appropriateness of injunctive relief on the merits. While courts in some jurisdictions have given effect to stipulated-irreparable-harm provisions, the Delaware Court of Chancery has expressed skepticism about such provisions, and Verdantis's position is that the question of irreparable harm should be determined by the court based on the facts and circumstances of the particular breach.
>
> **No bond waiver.** The NDA must **not waive the requirement to post a bond** or other security as a condition to obtaining a temporary restraining order or preliminary injunction. Under Delaware Chancery Court Rule 65 and analogous rules in other jurisdictions, a party seeking a temporary restraining order or preliminary injunction may be required to post a bond or other security to compensate the restrained party for any damages sustained if the injunction is later determined to have been wrongfully issued. Bond waivers deprive the restrained party of this critical procedural protection and create the risk that a party may obtain injunctive relief — with potentially severe commercial consequences for the restrained party — without bearing any risk of liability if the injunction is later vacated. Bond waivers are disfavored in Delaware courts, and Verdantis's policy is to resist their inclusion in any M&amp;A NDA.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept an equitable-relief provision that includes an acknowledgment that the parties "recognize the potential for irreparable harm" in the event of a breach, provided that the operative injunctive-relief clause still requires the moving party to satisfy the applicable legal standard for injunctive relief in the relevant jurisdiction, including demonstrating irreparable harm to the satisfaction of the court and posting a bond or other security if required by the court. This formulation is a permissible middle ground between a full stipulation of irreparable harm (which is unacceptable) and a purely silent NDA (which may make it more difficult for the moving party to obtain expedited injunctive relief). The key distinction is between an acknowledgment of potential harm (which is factual and hortatory) and a stipulation of actual harm (which is legal and dispositive).

**Walk-Away Position.** The following provisions are classified as **High Risk** deviations, each requiring approval from the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) and the VP, Corporate Development (Prescott):

> • **Stipulated irreparable harm.** Any provision in which the parties agree, stipulate, acknowledge, or consent that a breach of the NDA will be deemed to constitute irreparable harm as a matter of law, entitling the non-breaching party to injunctive relief without the need to demonstrate irreparable harm to the court. This includes formulations such as "the parties agree that a breach of this Agreement will cause irreparable harm for which monetary damages would be an inadequate remedy."
>
> • **Bond waiver.** Any provision in which the parties waive, or agree not to require, the posting of a bond or other security as a condition to the issuance of a temporary restraining order or preliminary injunction. This includes formulations such as "no bond or other security shall be required in connection with any application for equitable relief" and "each party waives any requirement for the securing or posting of any bond."

Outside counsel must redline any counterparty draft containing stipulated-irreparable-harm or bond-waiver provisions and must explain to counterparty counsel that these provisions are inconsistent with Verdantis's standard negotiation posture and its assessment of appropriate procedural protections in the injunctive-relief context.

**Section 9 — Governing Law and Dispute Resolution**

**Section 9.1 — Governing Law and Forum**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the **State of Delaware**, without regard to its conflicts-of-laws principles that would result in the application of the laws of any other jurisdiction. Each party shall submit to the exclusive jurisdiction and venue of the **Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware** for any action, suit, or proceeding arising out of or relating to the NDA. If the Court of Chancery declines to exercise jurisdiction over a particular matter, the parties shall submit to the exclusive jurisdiction of the **United States District Court for the District of Delaware**.

The selection of Delaware law and the Court of Chancery reflects several important considerations. Verdantis is incorporated in the State of Delaware, and the vast majority of its corporate governance matters are governed by Delaware law. The Delaware Court of Chancery is widely recognized as the preeminent forum for the resolution of corporate and commercial disputes, offering a highly experienced bench, well-developed and predictable jurisprudence on confidentiality, trade-secret, and M&amp;A-related matters, and efficient case management. The Court of Chancery's expertise in equity — including injunctive relief, specific performance, and other equitable remedies — makes it a particularly appropriate forum for disputes arising under M&amp;A NDAs, where the primary remedial concern is often the need for expedited equitable relief to prevent or enjoin a breach.

**Fallback Position.** If the counterparty insists on an alternative jurisdiction and Verdantis determines that the commercial importance of the Transaction warrants a concession on governing law and forum, New York law and the courts of the State of New York (specifically, the Supreme Court of the State of New York, New York County, or the United States District Court for the Southern District of New York) are acceptable alternatives. New York provides a well-developed body of commercial law and a sophisticated judiciary with extensive experience in M&amp;A-related disputes, making it a commercially reasonable alternative to Delaware. Acceptance of New York as the governing law and forum is classified as a **Low Risk** deviation and may be approved by the Associate General Counsel (Castellano), subject to General Counsel concurrence if the counterparty also seeks other deviations.

**Walk-Away Position.** The following are unacceptable as governing law or forum provisions:

> • Governing law of a jurisdiction with limited M&amp;A case law, unpredictable or underdeveloped equitable-remedy frameworks, or significant procedural disadvantages for Verdantis;
>
> • Governing law of a foreign jurisdiction (i.e., outside the United States), unless the Transaction involves a cross-border element that makes a foreign governing law unavoidable and the General Counsel (Ramaswamy) has approved the deviation;
>
> • Mandatory arbitration as the exclusive dispute-resolution mechanism for disputes arising under the NDA, particularly for disputes involving claims for injunctive or equitable relief. Mandatory arbitration may be acceptable for damages claims if the counterparty insists, but Verdantis must preserve the right to seek injunctive relief in a court of competent jurisdiction without first exhausting arbitration procedures.

**Section 9.2 — Waiver of Jury Trial**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA should include a mutual waiver of the right to a trial by jury for any action, suit, or proceeding arising out of or relating to the NDA. Jury-trial waivers are market-standard in M&amp;A NDAs and reduce the risk of unpredictable damages awards in the event of litigation. A mutual jury-trial waiver benefits both parties equally.

**Fallback Position.** If the counterparty is unwilling to include a jury-trial waiver, Verdantis may accept an NDA that is silent on the issue. Silence on jury-trial waiver is a **Low Risk** deviation and does not require escalation.

**Walk-Away Position.** Not applicable. A jury-trial waiver is preferred but is not a mandatory provision, and its absence does not constitute a walk-away position.

**Section 10 — Miscellaneous Provisions**

The miscellaneous provisions of the NDA address standard contractual terms that, while individually less significant than the substantive confidentiality and remedies provisions, collectively contribute to the enforceability, clarity, and administrability of the agreement.

**Section 10.1 — Assignment**

**Preferred Position.** The NDA shall provide that neither party may assign the NDA or any of its rights or obligations hereunder without the prior written consent of the other party, which consent may be withheld in the non-assigning party's sole discretion, except that either party may assign the NDA, without the consent of the other party, to a successor entity in connection with a merger, consolidation, reorganization, or sale of all or substantially all of the assigning party's assets to which the NDA relates. This successor-assignment carve-out ensures that the NDA survives a change-of-control transaction involving either party without the need to renegotiate the confidentiality arrangements.

**Fallback Position.** Verdantis may accept a provision requiring the prior written consent of the other party for all assignments, including assignments to successors in connection with a change-of-control transaction. This formulation provides the non-assigning party with greater control over the identity of its contractual counterparty but may create practical difficulties in the context of a rapid-closing M&amp;A transaction. This is a **Low Risk** deviation.

**Walk-Away Position.** An NDA that permits unrestricted assignment by either party — i.e., that allows a party to assign the NDA to any third party without the consent of the other party and without limitation to change-of-control successors — is unacceptable. Unrestricted assignment could result in Verdantis's Confidential Information being subject to an NDA with a party that Verdantis did not agree to deal with and that may not be bound by the same commercial understandings and incentives that animated the original NDA relationship.

**Section 10.2 — Amendment and Waiver**

The NDA shall provide that no amendment, modification, or supplement to the NDA shall be effective unless set forth in a written instrument signed by both parties. The NDA shall further provide that no waiver of any provision of the NDA shall be effective unless set forth in a written instrument signed by the waiving party, and that no waiver of any provision shall be deemed a waiver of any other provision or a continuing waiver of the same provision. These requirements ensure that the terms of the NDA can be modified or waived only through deliberate, documented action, and protect against claims that a party's conduct constituted an implied amendment or waiver.

**Section 10.3 — Entire Agreement**

The NDA shall include a standard entire-agreement clause providing that the NDA constitutes the entire agreement between the parties with respect to the subject matter hereof and supersedes all prior and contemporaneous negotiations, representations, understandings, and agreements, whether written or oral, relating to the subject matter of the NDA. The entire-agreement clause should be carefully reviewed to ensure that it does not inadvertently supersede any separate agreement between the parties that is intended to remain in effect (e.g., a pre-existing commercial agreement or services agreement). If such separate agreements exist, outside counsel should include appropriate savings language.

**Section 10.4 — Notices**

**Preferred Position.** All notices, requests, demands, and other communications required or permitted under the NDA shall be in writing and shall be delivered personally, by nationally recognized overnight courier service, or by certified mail (return receipt requested), and shall be deemed given upon receipt. Notices to Verdantis should be directed to the following address:

>

With a copy (which shall not constitute notice) to outside counsel as designated by Verdantis in writing from time to time.

Notices to the counterparty shall be directed to the address specified in the NDA or such other address as the counterparty may designate by written notice in accordance with the NDA's notice provisions.

Outside counsel should ensure that the NDA permits the designation of an updated notice address by written notice and that the notice provisions are reciprocal, applying equally to both parties.

**Appendix B — Negotiation Guidance Notes for Outside Counsel**

This Appendix sets forth general guidance for outside counsel engaged by Verdantis Solutions Inc. to review, redline, and negotiate M&amp;A NDAs on Verdantis's behalf. Outside counsel should review this guidance in conjunction with the substantive positions set forth in the body of the playbook and should direct any questions regarding the interpretation or application of the playbook to the playbook custodian, Associate General Counsel Lena F. Castellano.

**Clause-by-Clause Issues Memorandum.** For each counterparty draft NDA received, outside counsel must prepare a clause-by-clause Issues Memorandum identifying every provision of the counterparty's draft that deviates from the Preferred Position set forth in this playbook. For each identified deviation, the Issues Memorandum must include: (i) a description of the deviation, with a quotation of the relevant counterparty language and a reference to the applicable playbook section; (ii) a risk-tier classification in accordance with Section 2.1; (iii) an assessment of the legal and commercial implications of the deviation for Verdantis; and (iv) a recommended negotiation approach — specifically, whether the deviation should be accepted (with the requisite approval), countered with specific alternative language (identifying the alternative), or rejected outright. The Issues Memorandum should be organized by risk tier, with High Risk deviations addressed first, followed by Medium Risk and Low Risk deviations. The Issues Memorandum should be delivered to the Verdantis legal team (specifically, to the playbook custodian and the deal lead) in draft form for review and comment before any redline is circulated to counterparty counsel.

**Redline Approach.** When preparing a redline of the counterparty's draft NDA, outside counsel should propose the Preferred Position set forth in this playbook in the first instance for every deviating provision. Outside counsel may propose the Fallback Position only after the counterparty has rejected the Preferred Position in a prior round of negotiation and the Verdantis legal team has authorized the fallback. Outside counsel should not preemptively concede to a fallback or walk-away position in the initial redline, even if outside counsel believes that the counterparty is likely to reject the Preferred Position, unless specifically instructed to do so by the Verdantis legal team.

**Walk-Away Communications.** Walk-Away Positions should be communicated to counterparty counsel only after the relevant internal approvals have been obtained in accordance with Section 2 and the deal team has confirmed its willingness to terminate negotiations on the basis of the provision in question. Outside counsel should not represent to counterparty counsel that a particular provision is a "deal-breaker" or "walk-away" issue without first receiving express authorization from the Verdantis deal team. Premature communication of walk-away positions can undermine negotiating leverage and may foreclose compromise positions that the deal team would otherwise be willing to explore.

**Coordination with Deal Team.** All communications with counterparty counsel regarding M&amp;A NDA negotiations should be coordinated with the Verdantis deal lead (currently VP, Corporate Development, Eamon J. Prescott) and the playbook custodian (Associate General Counsel, Lena F. Castellano). Outside counsel should copy the deal lead and playbook custodian on all substantive correspondence with counterparty counsel, including redlines, cover letters, and issue-specific emails. Outside counsel should not engage in substantive oral negotiations with counterparty counsel without first obtaining a mandate from the Verdantis deal team regarding the positions to be advocated and the concessions, if any, that have been authorized.

**Timeline Expectations.** The Verdantis deal team expects a first-round redline and accompanying Issues Memorandum to be delivered to the Verdantis legal team within **5 business days** of receipt of the counterparty's draft NDA. If outside counsel anticipates that additional time will be required due to the complexity of the counterparty's draft, the volume of deviations, or other factors, outside counsel should notify the playbook custodian promptly and propose a revised timeline. For second-round and subsequent redlines, outside counsel should target a turnaround of **3 business days** from receipt of the counterparty's revised draft, unless otherwise agreed with the Verdantis legal team.

**Confidentiality of Playbook.** This playbook is a privileged and confidential work product of Verdantis Solutions Inc. Outside counsel must not disclose the existence or contents of this playbook to any counterparty, counterparty counsel, or any third party. Outside counsel must not reference this playbook in any communication with counterparty counsel or in any redline or negotiation document circulated to the counterparty. The negotiation positions set forth in this playbook should be communicated to counterparty counsel as Verdantis's positions, without attribution to or reference to any internal playbook or policy document.

**Retention and Updates.** Outside counsel should retain a copy of the current version of this playbook in their matter files for the duration of their engagement on Verdantis M&amp;A matters. Outside counsel should confirm with the playbook custodian at the commencement of each new NDA engagement that they are working from the most current version of the playbook. When a new version of the playbook is issued, outside counsel should destroy or return all copies of the superseded version and replace them with the current version.
